waking up

What you are looking at

This is a real fruit-fly circuit, running live. 448 neurons and 20,901 synapses, traced from an actual Drosophila brain by electron microscope. When the proboscis comes out it is because MN9 — the motor neuron that extends it — just fired.

How to play

  • Give it sugar. 21 taste neurons get stimulated. Watch the wave cross the circuit and reach MN9.
  • Let it get hungry. Full to starving takes 4 hours of real time, browser closed or not.
  • Let it out on the web and it walks over the page you are reading. Move your pointer at it fast and it bolts — that is a looming stimulus.
  • Click any neuron to silence it and watch the MN9 trace below. Press Give it its neurons back to undo.

The network view

Each dot is a neuron; each faint line is a real synapse. When a neuron fires, the connections leaving it light up — blue if excitatory, pink if inhibitory. The layout groups neurons by who they talk to; it is not anatomy.

Why your fly is unique

On install you got a seed, and the seed silenced 40 neurons. About a third of this circuit is inhibitory — neurons whose job is to stop the fly eating. So knocking out 40 at random sometimes cripples it, and sometimes removes the brakes and produces a fly that eats better than a normal one. Measured over 8 seeds: 2 better, 3 worse, 3 the same. Nobody designed that.

What is real and what is not

Real: the neurons, the synapses, MN9 in Hz, the proboscis, your mutation.
Not real: the hunger clock, the walking and the wingbeat (good animation, but animation), and the artwork, which is AI-generated illustration.
